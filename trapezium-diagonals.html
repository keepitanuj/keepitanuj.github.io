trapezium and its diagonals

quant notes · geometry · q.8

problem

ABCD is a trapezium in which AB is parallel to DC and its diagonals intersect at P. If AP = (3x − 1) cm, PC = (5x − 3) cm, BP = (2x + 1) cm and PD = (6x − 5) cm, then the length of DB is:

  • (a) 10 cm
  • (b) 16 cm
  • (c) 14 cm
  • (d) 12 cm

AB ∥ DC. Sketch not to scale.

solution

In this question, we are solving for DB, which is BP + PD:

DB = BP + PD = (2x + 1) + (6x − 5) = 8x − 4

Now, observing the options closely: if I add 4 to the answer, it must be a multiple of 8.

(a) 10 + 4 = 14   (b) 16 + 4 = 20   (c) 14 + 4 = 18   (d) 12 + 4 = 16

Only 16 is a multiple of 8, which is possible only in option (d).

answer: (d) 12 cm

check

The shortcut assumes x is a whole number. To confirm it: the diagonals of a trapezium divide each other in the same ratio, so AP/PC = BP/PD. Cross-multiplying gives (3x − 1)(6x − 5) = (5x − 3)(2x + 1), which simplifies to 2x² − 5x + 2 = 0, so x = 2 or x = ½. At x = ½, PC would be negative, so x = 2 and DB = 8(2) − 4 = 12 cm.
