i tried to go a week without knowing what time it was

i covered every clock i own with tape. phone in grayscale, lock screen clock hidden, laptop menu bar turned off. microwave unplugged, which in hindsight was the real sacrifice.

the rule was simple: for seven days i'm not allowed to know the time. i can know if it's light or dark. i can know if i'm hungry. that's it.

day 1it's fine, actually

the first day felt like a holiday. i woke up when i woke up. i ate when i was hungry. i felt smug in the specific way you feel smug on the first day of anything.

day 3i eat when i'm bored

this was the big one. without a clock telling me it was "lunchtime" i expected to eat less. i ate more. it turns out i don't eat when i'm hungry, i eat when i don't know what to do next, and without time to structure the day i never knew what to do next.

time isn't something i keep. it's something that keeps me.

that sounds deep. i wrote it at what i later found out was 4:12am.

day 6i broke

someone asked me to meet "at 6" and i realised i had no way to do that without cheating. so i cheated. it was 5:51. i had been, by pure vibes, almost exactly right.

noteswhat i'd tell you if you tried it

  • tell people first. they get weird about it otherwise.
  • you will look at your wrist even if you've never worn a watch.
  • sunsets get very important, very fast.
  • don't unplug the microwave.1

1. you can't heat things in it, obviously, but mostly the beep was the only thing in the flat that sounded like it knew what it was doing.
